Kendall's Notation Breakdown

The standard notation (M/M/s):(ꝏ/FIFO) represents a multi-server Markovian queuing model:

  • M (Arrival Process): Poisson arrivals with constant rate $\lambda$. Inter-arrival times are independent and exponentially distributed with mean $1/\lambda$.
  • M (Service Distribution): Exponential service times with parameter $\mu$ per server. Mean service time per customer is $1/\mu$.
  • s (Server Count): $s$ identical parallel servers working independently. When $k \le s$ customers are in the facility, service rate is $k\mu$. When $k > s$, total service rate is $s\mu$.
  • ꝏ (System Capacity): Infinite waiting room. Arriving customers never balk or leave due to full capacity.
  • FIFO (Queue Discipline): First-In, First-Out. Waiting customers are served strictly in order of arrival by the next available server.
Key Distinction from M/M/1: A single queue feeds $s$ parallel servers. Utilization is distributed across the server pool: $$\rho = \frac{\lambda}{s\mu}$$

Mathematical Formulas Reference

Derived from continuous-time Birth-Death processes with transition rates $\lambda_n = \lambda$ and $\mu_n = \min(n, s)\mu$:

1. Probability of System Empty ($P_0$): $$P_0 = \left[ \sum_{n=0}^{s-1} \frac{(\lambda/\mu)^n}{n!} + \frac{(\lambda/\mu)^s}{s!(1 - \rho)} \right]^{-1}$$
2. Probability of Waiting (Erlang's C Formula): $$P(\text{wait}) = C(s, r) = \frac{(\lambda/\mu)^s}{s!(1 - \rho)} P_0$$
3. Average Customers in Queue ($L_q$) & System ($L_s$): $$L_q = \frac{P(\text{wait}) \cdot \rho}{1 - \rho}, \quad L_s = L_q + \frac{\lambda}{\mu}$$
4. Average Waiting Times (Little's Law): $$W_q = \frac{L_q}{\lambda} = \frac{P(\text{wait})}{s\mu - \lambda}, \quad W_s = W_q + \frac{1}{\mu}$$

Stability Condition ($\lambda < s\mu$)

For an $(M/M/s):(\infty/\text{FIFO})$ queue, a stationary steady-state distribution exists if and only if:

$$\rho = \frac{\lambda}{s\mu} < 1 \iff \lambda < s\mu$$

Why does $\lambda \ge s\mu$ cause infinite queue explosion?

The maximum processing capacity of the entire facility is $s\mu$. If $\lambda > s\mu$, arrivals occur faster on average than the facility can process, causing backlog to accumulate continuously without limit. Even if $\lambda = s\mu$ (critical boundary $\rho = 1.00$), stochastic random fluctuations ensure that whenever a server experiences idle time, that lost capacity can never be regained. Consequently, the queue variance diverges to infinity, and steady state cannot exist.
