User Guide & Theory Documentation

Complete manual explaining Kendall's notation, formulas, stability conditions, and operational steps.

Kendall's Notation Breakdown

The standard notation (M/M/1):(∞/FIFO) describes a fundamental single-server queuing system characterized by:

  • M (First position - Arrival Process): Markovian / Poisson arrival process with rate $\lambda$. Inter-arrival times follow an exponential distribution $A(t) = 1 - e^{-\lambda t}$ with mean $1/\lambda$.
  • M (Second position - Service Mechanism): Markovian / Exponential service times with parameter $\mu$. Mean service time is $1/\mu$.
  • 1 (Third position - Server Count): A single service counter or processor serves incoming customers one at a time.
  • ∞ (Fourth position - System Capacity): Infinite buffer / waiting room. No customers are turned away or balk due to a full queue.
  • FIFO (Fifth position - Queue Discipline): First-In, First-Out. The customer who arrives first receives service first.
Memoryless Property: Both arrival and service events are memoryless: $$P(X > s + t \mid X > s) = P(X > t)$$

Mathematical Formulas Reference

All performance measures in this calculator are derived using continuous-time Markov chains (Birth-Death processes) in steady state:

1. Traffic Intensity / Utilisation Factor: $$\rho = \frac{\lambda}{\mu}$$
2. Probability of Empty System: $$P_0 = 1 - \rho = 1 - \frac{\lambda}{\mu}$$
3. Average Number in System and Queue: $$L = \frac{\lambda}{\mu - \lambda} = \frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}, \quad L_q = \frac{\lambda^2}{\mu(\mu - \lambda)} = L - \rho$$
4. Average Waiting Times (Little's Law): $$W = \frac{1}{\mu - \lambda} = \frac{L}{\lambda}, \quad W_q = \frac{\lambda}{\mu(\mu - \lambda)} = W - \frac{1}{\mu}$$

Stability Condition ($\lambda < \mu$)

For an $(M/M/1):(\infty/\text{FIFO})$ queue, a stationary steady-state distribution exists if and only if:

$$\rho = \frac{\lambda}{\mu} < 1 \iff \lambda < \mu$$

What happens when $\lambda \ge \mu$?

If $\lambda \ge \mu$, customers arrive at or faster than the maximum capacity of the server. Because random variations exist, even if $\lambda = \mu$, idle gaps cannot be "saved" for future surges. As time $t \to \infty$, the expected number of customers in the queue $L_q \to \infty$ and waiting time $W_q \to \infty$. The Markov chain is transient ($\lambda > \mu$) or null-recurrent ($\lambda = \mu$), and the sum of state probabilities $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P_n$ diverges.

How to Use the Calculator

  1. Enter Arrival & Service Rates Specify $\lambda$ (arrival rate) and $\mu$ (service rate) using the input boxes or fine-tuning sliders. Ensure both values are strictly positive real numbers ($> 0$).
  2. Choose Units or Presets Switch time units (Hours, Minutes, Seconds) or click any preset (e.g., Bank Teller, Web Server) to load realistic domain figures.
  3. Observe 3D Queue Simulation Watch the 3D stage simulate actual arrivals and departures in real-time. Use orbit controls (click and drag) to inspect the queue from any angle.
  4. Review Metrics & Little's Law Analyze $\rho, P_0, L, L_q, W, W_q$. Check the Little's Law badges confirming $L = \lambda W$ and $L_q = \lambda W_q$.
  5. Run & Verify Test Cases Scroll to the Test Cases table. Click "Run All Test Cases" or test individual rows with "Load & Run" to confirm mathematical accuracy.
